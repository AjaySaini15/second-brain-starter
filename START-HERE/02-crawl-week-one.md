# Crawl — week one

**Goal:** make the AI useful daily with just a chat tool — and let it maintain its own memory. 15 minutes a day, no new software, no typing files.

## The daily loop
1. Bring it one real piece of work every day — notes to structure, an email to draft, a decision to think through. Dictate the messy version; real work only.
2. When it gets something wrong, correct it **and accept the file-update it proposes.** (The CLAUDE-starter rules make it offer the exact line — "Add to CONTEXT: X reports to Y?" — you just say yes.) In a chat tool, paste the proposed lines back into your project instructions at week's end; that 2-minute re-paste is your only manual chore at this level.
3. End each work session with: "Anything from today worth remembering? Propose MEMORY lines." Approve what's right.

## Add your second skill — by asking, not writing
When one loop feels natural, run your week's notes through `skills/weekly-review.md` on Friday. Then try the move that matters: do any recurring task with it once, and say **"write this up as a skill file I can reuse."** It drafts the skill; you correct it by talking. You'll never hand-write a skill in your life.

## What to expect (honestly)
This week costs you MORE time than it saves. That's normal — you're doing reps, and it's learning you. It's a workout: muscles hurt first, results show later. The people who quit in week one expected a vending machine.

## By Sunday you should have
- CONTEXT.md grown 2× — almost none of it typed by you
- Two or three skills, at least one drafted by the AI itself
- The correction reflex: never fix just the answer, always accept the memory update

**Then:** `03-walk-first-month.md` — where the AI gets its own hands on the files.
